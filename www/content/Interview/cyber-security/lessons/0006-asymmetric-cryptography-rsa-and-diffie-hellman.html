1 · The One Idea

Symmetric encryption suffers from the Key Distribution Dilemma: if Alice and Bob have never met before, how can they securely exchange a secret symmetric key over a public internet monitored by eavesdroppers? Asymmetric (Public Key) Cryptography solves this with key pairs: a Public Key (which anyone can see) and a mathematically related Private Key (kept strictly secret). What the public key encrypts, ONLY the private key can decrypt.

3 · Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange Explained

Diffie-Hellman allows two parties to create a shared secret key without ever sending the secret itself:

  1. Alice and Bob agree on public parameters: a large prime p and a base generator g.
  2. Alice picks private secret a; sends public A = ga mod p.
  3. Bob picks private secret b; sends public B = gb mod p.
  4. Alice computes: S = Ba mod p = (gb)a mod p.
  5. Bob computes: S = Ab mod p = (ga)b mod p.
  6. Both arrive at identical shared secret S = gab mod p! An eavesdropper observing A and B cannot compute S due to the Discrete Logarithm Problem.

4 · The RSA Algorithm Step-by-Step

RSA Mathematical Proof & Procedure

  1. Choose two distinct large prime numbers p and q.
  2. Compute modulus n = p × q.
  3. Compute Euler's Totient: φ(n) = (p - 1)(q - 1).
  4. Choose public exponent e such that 1 < e < φ(n) and gcd(e, φ(n)) = 1 (standard value: 65537).
  5. Calculate private exponent d as the modular multiplicative inverse: d ≡ e-1 (mod φ(n)).
  6. Public Key: (e, n) | Private Key: (d, n).
  7. Encryption: Ciphertext c = me mod n.
  8. Decryption: Plaintext m = cd mod n.

Security relies on the fact that multiplying two 2048-bit primes takes microseconds, but factoring n back into p and q takes billions of years on supercomputers.

5 · Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC)

While RSA requires 3072-bit or 4096-bit keys for modern security, Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC) achieves the identical security strength with just a 256-bit key (e.g. Curve25519, Secp256k1). Based on the algebraic structure of elliptic curves over finite fields (y2 = x3 + ax + b), ECC provides dramatically faster signatures, smaller certificates, and lower battery consumption on mobile devices.

1 · The One Idea

Security is not a product you buy or a checkbox you tick; it is a continuous risk-management process. An attacker only needs to find one single unpatched flaw to compromise an entire company, while defenders must secure every single port, library, endpoint, and employee. To make security systematic and defensible, engineers use the CIA Triad, the Defense-in-Depth model, and formal Threat Modeling frameworks (STRIDE) to identify and mitigate attack vectors before writing code.

2 · The CIA Triad & Non-Repudiation

C

Confidentiality

Data is concealed from unauthorized individuals, entities, or processes. Enforced via encryption (AES-256, TLS 1.3), access controls (RBAC), and data classification.

I

Integrity

Data cannot be modified, tampered with, or corrupted by unauthorized actors undetected. Enforced via cryptographic hashes (SHA-256), digital signatures, and HMACs.

A

Availability

Authorized users have timely and dependable access to data and resources when needed. Protected against Denial of Service (DDoS) via redundancy, load balancers, and failover clustering.

Non-Repudiation & The AAA Model

  • Non-Repudiation: Guarantees that a sender cannot deny having sent a message (proven through Digital Signatures and immutable audit logs).
  • Authentication (AuthN): Verifying identity ("Who are you?").
  • Authorization (AuthZ): Granting specific permissions ("What are you allowed to do?").
  • Accounting / Auditing: Tracking historical actions ("What did you do and when?").

3 · Threat Modeling with STRIDE

Developed by Praerit Garg and Loren Kohnfelder at Microsoft, STRIDE categorizes security threats against system components:

ThreatViolated PropertyDefinition & Real-World ExamplePrimary Defense
SpoofingAuthenticityPretending to be someone or something else (e.g. IP spoofing, stolen session cookie).Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), TLS mutual certificates.
TamperingIntegrityModifying data in transit or storage (e.g. altering SQL parameters, MITM packet edit).Hashes, HMAC, Digital Signatures, TLS encryption.
RepudiationNon-RepudiationDenying an action occurred without proof (e.g. "I never transferred that $10,000").Cryptographic audit trails, centralized SIEM logging.
Information DisclosureConfidentialityExposing sensitive data to unauthorized parties (e.g. path traversal leaking /etc/shadow).Encryption at rest and in transit, strict authorization checks.
Denial of ServiceAvailabilityExhausting system resources to make services unavailable (e.g. SYN flood, slowloris).Rate limiting, Cloudflare DDoS scrubbing, CDN caching.
Elevation of PrivilegeAuthorizationGaining capabilities beyond authorized role (e.g. low-priv user popping root via SUID bug).Principle of Least Privilege, memory sandboxing, AppArmor/SELinux.

4 · Zero Trust Architecture: "Never Trust, Always Verify"

Traditional "castle-and-moat" security assumed that anything inside the internal corporate network was trustworthy. In modern cloud architecture, attackers routinely breach perimeters (phishing, VPN compromises). Zero Trust mandates that every request—whether originating from the public internet or an internal workstation—must be explicitly authenticated, authorized, and encrypted before access is granted.

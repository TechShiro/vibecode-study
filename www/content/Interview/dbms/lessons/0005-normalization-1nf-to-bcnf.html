1 · The One Idea

Poor database schemas suffer from three devastating modification anomalies:

  • Update Anomaly: If an employee's department address is duplicated in 1,000 employee records, changing the office location requires 1,000 updates. If one fails, data is corrupted.
  • Insertion Anomaly: You cannot record a new department's details until at least one employee is hired, because emp_id is part of the primary key and cannot be NULL.
  • Deletion Anomaly: If the last employee in a department resigns and their row is deleted, the department's existence and office location are wiped from the system permanently.

Database Normalization is the formal process of decomposing tables with redundancy into smaller, well-structured relations without losing data (Lossless Join) or constraints (Dependency Preservation).

4 · Detailed Breakdown of Normal Forms

Normal FormRequirement / ConditionViolating PatternRemedy
1NF (First Normal Form) All attribute values must be atomic (indivisible scalars). No multi-valued attributes, no arrays, no nested tables. phone_numbers = "98765, 87654" in a single column. Separate into individual rows or a dedicated child table.
2NF (Second Normal Form) Must be in 1NF AND have NO Partial Dependencies: no non-prime attribute may depend on a proper subset of any candidate key. In {student_id, course_id} → course_fee, where course_fee depends solely on course_id. Decompose into separate relations: (student_id, course_id) and (course_id, course_fee).
3NF (Third Normal Form) Must be in 2NF AND have NO Transitive Dependencies. For every non-trivial FD X → Y: either X is a Superkey OR Y is a Prime Attribute (part of a candidate key). emp_id → dept_id and dept_id → dept_name. Non-prime dept_name transitively depends on emp_id. Decompose into Employee(emp_id, dept_id) and Department(dept_id, dept_name).
BCNF (Boyce-Codd Normal Form) Stricter 3NF. For EVERY non-trivial FD X → Y, X MUST be a Superkey without exception. A relation with overlapping candidate keys where a prime attribute is determined by a non-superkey. Decompose relation into (X, Y) and (R - Y).

5 · Decomposition Guarantees: Lossless vs Dependency Preserving

Lossless Join Decomposition

A decomposition of R into R1 and R2 is guaranteed to be lossless if and only if their shared attributes form a superkey of at least one of the decomposed tables: (R1 ∩ R2 → R1) OR (R1 ∩ R2 → R2). Lossless property is MANDATORY; otherwise, fake "spurious tuples" are generated when joining tables back.

Dependency Preservation

A decomposition is dependency preserving if the union of FDs enforceable on individual tables equals the original FD set F: (F1 ∪ F2)+ = F+. Every relation can always be decomposed into 3NF with both Lossless Join AND Dependency Preservation. However, BCNF guarantees Lossless Join, but cannot always preserve all dependencies.

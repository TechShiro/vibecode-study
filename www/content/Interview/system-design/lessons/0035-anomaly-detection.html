Anomaly Detection, SLIs, SLOs & Error Budgets

Google Site Reliability Engineering (SRE) frameworks for setting alerts that matter.

↑ Back to Course Index Module I — Monitoring & Logging

1 · The One Idea

If you alert an on-call engineer at 3:00 AM every time CPU usage blips to 82% for five seconds, the team will develop Alert Fatigue — they will ignore notifications, silence PagerDuty, and eventually sleep through a catastrophic database crash. SRE engineering prevents this by tying alerts strictly to user happiness and business impact using SLIs and SLOs.

2 · SLI vs SLO vs SLA: The Golden Triangle

Term What It Is Concrete Example
SLI (Service Level Indicator) The exact quantitative measurement of compliance. What is actually happening right now. "99.85% of successful HTTP requests returned within < 200ms over the last 30 days."
SLO (Service Level Objective) The internal target agreed upon by engineering and product teams. "We aim for 99.9% of requests to complete within < 200ms."
SLA (Service Level Agreement) The formal legal and business contract with paying customers, including financial penalties or refunds if missed. "If availability drops below 99.5%, customer receives a 20% billing credit." (SLO is always stricter than SLA!)

3 · The Error Budget: Balancing Velocity and Stability

If your SLO is 99.9% uptime, your Error Budget is 0.1% (which equals 43.8 minutes of allowable downtime per month).

  • Budget Remaining: If the system is healthy and error budget is green, engineers are free to ship new features aggressively.
  • Budget Burned: If recent outages burn through the 0.1% budget, all feature deployments are strictly frozen! 100% of engineering bandwidth shifts to bug fixing, refactoring, and stability work until the budget recovers.

4 · Threshold vs Statistical Anomaly Detection

  • Static Thresholds: Alert if error_rate > 5%. Fragile: what if Sunday traffic is 1/10th of Friday traffic?
  • Statistical Anomaly Detection: Evaluates deviations using standard deviations (Z-score) or machine learning algorithms (Holt-Winters seasonal forecasting). Automatically accounts for diurnal traffic waves (day vs night traffic)!

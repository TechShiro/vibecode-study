What is a Genetic Algorithm?

Developed by John H. Holland (1975) and popularized by David E. Goldberg, a Genetic Algorithm (GA) is an adaptive heuristic search and optimization algorithm grounded in Charles Darwin's principles of natural evolution — “survival of the fittest”.

Unlike calculus-based methods that require gradient information (and thus stumble on discontinuous, non-differentiable, or multimodal landscapes), GAs operate on a population of candidate solutions simultaneously, exploring multiple regions of the search space via stochastic operators.

Fitness Function

The fitness function assesses the quality or performance of each candidate solution. It guides the survival and reproduction mechanism:

  • For a maximization problem: fitness is often directly proportional to the objective function, e.g., F(x) = f(x) (with shifting if values are negative).
  • For a minimization problem (cost, error): fitness must invert the objective, e.g., F(x) = 1 / (1 + f(x)) or F(x) = M - f(x) where M is an upper bound.

Selection Methods (Reproduction)

Selection allocates reproductive trials to chromosomes based on their fitness. Fitter chromosomes get more copies in the mating pool, while weaker ones tend to die out.

1. Roulette Wheel Selection (Fitness Proportionate)

Each chromosome is allocated a slice of a circular roulette wheel proportional to its fitness score. The wheel is spun N times to select N parents.

Selection Probability Formula Pi = fi / Σj=1N fj
Expected Count: ei = fi / f̄   (where f̄ = mean population fitness)
Worked selection example

Consider a population of 4 chromosomes with the following fitness values:

  • Chromosome 1: f₁ = 15
  • Chromosome 2: f₂ = 25
  • Chromosome 3: f₃ = 10
  • Chromosome 4: f₄ = 50

Total Fitness: Σf = 15 + 25 + 10 + 50 = 100.

2. Tournament Selection

Randomly pick k individuals (tournament size, e.g., k = 2 or 3) from the population. The one with the highest fitness among the k contestants wins and enters the mating pool. Repeat until the pool is full. Advantage: very fast O(k), no need to compute global population sum, works naturally in parallel.

3. Rank Selection

Chromosomes are sorted in ascending order of fitness and assigned ranks from 1 (worst) to N (best). Selection probabilities are calculated from their ranks rather than raw fitness scores. This prevents a “super-individual” from dominating early generations and stalling diversity.

4. Elitism

Standard selection and crossover can accidentally destroy the best solution found so far. Elitism copies the top 1 or 2 highest-performing individuals directly into the next generation without modification, ensuring the maximum fitness never decreases across iterations.

Crossover (Recombination) Operators

Crossover combines genetic material from two parent chromosomes to produce offspring, exploiting promising building blocks (schemas). It is applied with a high probability p_c (typically 0.60 – 0.95).

Crossover OperatorMechanismSchematic Example
Single-Point Crossover A single crossover site is randomly chosen along the string; bits beyond this site are swapped between parents. Parent 1: 1 1 0 | 0 1 → Child 1: 1 1 0 1 0
Parent 2: 0 0 1 | 1 0 → Child 2: 0 0 1 0 1
Two-Point Crossover Two cut points are chosen; the segment between the two points is swapped. Reduces positional bias. Parent 1: 1 1 | 0 0 | 1 → Child 1: 1 1 1 1 1
Parent 2: 0 0 | 1 1 | 0 → Child 2: 0 0 0 0 0
Uniform Crossover Each bit position is decided independently based on a random mask (e.g., coin toss probability 0.5). Mask: 1 0 1 0 0 (1=P1, 0=P2)
Child 1 gets bits {1, 3} from P1, and bits {2, 4, 5} from P2.

Mutation Operators

Mutation alters one or more gene values in a chromosome with a small probability p_m (typically 0.001 – 0.05). Its critical purpose is to maintain genetic diversity and recover beneficial alleles that may have been lost during selection, preventing the population from getting permanently stuck in local optima.

  • Bit-Flip Mutation (Binary): Inverts a bit (0 → 1 or 1 → 0).
  • Swap Mutation (Permutation): Randomly picks two gene positions and exchanges their values.
  • Inversion Mutation: Picks a contiguous subarray of genes and reverses their order.

Convergence & Stopping Criteria

A genetic algorithm runs until one or more termination criteria are met:

  1. Maximum generations reached (e.g., 500 or 1000 iterations).
  2. Target fitness achieved (e.g., error falls below 10−4).
  3. Population stagnation: the average fitness becomes nearly identical to the maximum fitness (diversity collapsed).
  4. No improvement over a fixed number of successive generations (stall limit).

Premature Convergence: Occurs when the population becomes uniform around a sub-optimal local peak early in the run. Common causes include an overly aggressive selection pressure, high crossover with near-zero mutation, or small population size.

Retrieval practice

1. In Roulette Wheel Selection, the probability of selecting chromosome i is proportional to:

Roulette wheel selection assigns slice sizes directly proportional to fitness ratios.

2. What is the primary role of the mutation operator in a Genetic Algorithm?

Crossover explores by recombining existing traits, whereas mutation ensures new or lost traits can re-enter the population.

3. Why is elitism used in genetic algorithms?

Without elitism, stochastic crossover and mutation could disrupt the highest fitness chromosome of generation t.

Recall without looking

Name four chromosome encoding methods and an example application of each.

Binary (knapsack/0-1 problems), Real-value (continuous parameter optimization, NN weights), Permutation (TSP, job sequencing), Tree (genetic programming, evolving code expressions).

What is the difference between Roulette Wheel Selection and Rank Selection?

Roulette wheel allocates selection probability based on raw fitness (P_i = f_i / Σf). Rank selection sorts individuals and assigns probability based on rank order (1 to N), preventing a super-fit individual from dominating the wheel and stalling diversity.

Write down the GA lifecycle sequence.

1. Initialize population → 2. Evaluate fitness → 3. Test termination condition → 4. Select parents → 5. Apply crossover (p_c) → 6. Apply mutation (p_m) → 7. Form next generation and repeat.

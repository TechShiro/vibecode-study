What is Particle Swarm Optimization?

Introduced by social psychologist James Kennedy and electrical engineer Russell C. Eberhart in 1995, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is a population-based stochastic optimization algorithm inspired by the social foraging behaviors of bird flocks and fish schools.

Unlike Genetic Algorithms which rely on evolutionary genetic operators (selection, crossover, mutation) to replace individuals across generations, PSO maintains a stable swarm of particles that “fly” through a multi-dimensional search space, adjusting their trajectories based on both their own individual experience and the collective knowledge of the entire swarm.

The Governing Mathematical Equations

In a D-dimensional search space, the motion of each particle i at time step t is governed by two fundamental update rules:

1. Velocity Update Equation vid(t+1) = w · vid(t) + c1 · r1 · [pbestid − xid(t)] + c2 · r2 · [gbestd − xid(t)]
2. Position Update Equation xid(t+1) = xid(t) + vid(t+1)

Deconstructing the Three Velocity Terms

  1. Inertia Term — w · vid(t): Represents the particle's previous momentum. The parameter w is the inertia weight (introduced by Shi & Eberhart, 1998). It controls the trade-off between global exploration and local exploitation:
    • High w (≈ 0.9): Encourages broad global exploration across wide areas.
    • Low w (≈ 0.4): Encourages fine local exploitation around promising regions.
    • Linearly Decreasing Inertia: Often adapted over iterations:
      w(t) = wmax − [(wmax − wmin) / Tmax] · t (typically transitioning from 0.9 → 0.4).
  2. Cognitive (Personal) Term — c1 · r1 · [pbestid − xid(t)]: Represents individual learning and nostalgia. It pulls the particle back toward its own best historical position (pbest).
    • c₁ is the cognitive acceleration coefficient (typically set to ≈ 2.0).
    • r₁ ∼ U(0, 1) is a uniform random number that injects stochastic unpredictability.
  3. Social (Swarm) Term — c2 · r2 · [gbestd − xid(t)]: Represents social cooperation and flock intelligence. It pulls the particle toward the best global solution found anywhere in the swarm (gbest).
    • c₂ is the social acceleration coefficient (typically set to ≈ 2.0).
    • r₂ ∼ U(0, 1) is an independent uniform random number.
Velocity clamping

To prevent particles from accelerating uncontrollably and overshooting the boundary of the feasible search space (“swarm explosion”), velocities are clamped within strict limits: −Vmax ≤ vid(t+1) ≤ Vmax.

Full Worked Numerical: One Iteration of PSO

Numerical problem

Objective: Minimize the 1-D quadratic function f(x) = x².
Swarm parameters: Inertia weight w = 0.7, acceleration coefficients c₁ = 1.5, c₂ = 1.5.
Random numbers for iteration 1: r₁ = 0.5, r₂ = 0.6.
Initial swarm (3 particles):

  • Particle 1: position x₁ = 2.0, velocity v₁ = 0.5
  • Particle 2: position x₂ = −3.0, velocity v₂ = −0.5
  • Particle 3: position x₃ = 4.0, velocity v₃ = 1.0

Step 1: Initial Fitness & Best Records

Evaluate initial fitness f(x) = x² for all particles (lower is better):

  • f(x₁) = 2.0² = 4.0 → pbest₁ = 2.0 (fitness 4.0)
  • f(x₂) = (−3.0)² = 9.0 → pbest₂ = −3.0 (fitness 9.0)
  • f(x₃) = 4.0² = 16.0 → pbest₃ = 4.0 (fitness 16.0)

The global minimum among all is pbest₁ = 2.0 with fitness 4.0 → gbest = 2.0.

Step 2: Velocity & Position Updates

Common multipliers: c₁ · r₁ = 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75, and c₂ · r₂ = 1.5 × 0.6 = 0.90.

Particle 1:

v₁(1) = 0.7(0.5) + 0.75(2.0 − 2.0) + 0.90(2.0 − 2.0) = 0.35 + 0 + 0 = 0.35
x₁(1) = 2.0 + 0.35 = 2.35
New fitness: f(2.35) = 2.35² = 5.5225 > 4.0 → pbest₁ remains 2.0.

Particle 2:

v₂(1) = 0.7(−0.5) + 0.75(−3.0 − (−3.0)) + 0.90(2.0 − (−3.0))
v₂(1) = −0.35 + 0 + 0.90(5.0) = −0.35 + 4.50 = 4.15
x₂(1) = −3.0 + 4.15 = 1.15
New fitness: f(1.15) = 1.15² = 1.3225 < 9.0 → pbest₂ updates to 1.15!

Particle 3:

v₃(1) = 0.7(1.0) + 0.75(4.0 − 4.0) + 0.90(2.0 − 4.0)
v₃(1) = 0.70 + 0 + 0.90(−2.0) = 0.70 − 1.80 = −1.10
x₃(1) = 4.0 − 1.10 = 2.90
New fitness: f(2.90) = 2.90² = 8.41 < 16.0 → pbest₃ updates to 2.90!

Step 3: Update Global Best (gbest)

Compare new personal bests: pbest₁ (4.0), pbest₂ (1.3225), pbest₃ (8.41).

The lowest value is 1.3225 from Particle 2 → New gbest = 1.15 (improved from 2.0). The swarm has rapidly migrated closer to the optimal coordinate x = 0 in a single iteration!

Comparison: PSO vs Genetic Algorithm (GA)

FeatureParticle Swarm Optimization (PSO)Genetic Algorithm (GA)
Natural InspirationSwarm behavior (bird flocking, fish schooling)Darwinian natural selection & genetics
Population DynamicsFixed population of particles that persist and adjust positionsSuccessive generations created via reproduction; parents discarded
Operators UsedArithmetic velocity and position updates (no crossover/mutation)Selection, Crossover (recombination), and Mutation
Internal MemoryYes — each particle remembers its own pbest and swarm gbestNo — population retains no individual memory across generations
Information SharingOne-way flow: gbest broadcasts the optimal location to all particlesTwo-way sharing: parents mutually exchange chromosome segments
Variables HandledNaturally continuous (real numbers); discrete versions require binarizationNaturally discrete/binary strings; real-coded versions require specialized operators
Computational ComplexityLow — fewer parameters to tune, very fast execution per stepHigher — requires string encoding/decoding, sorting, and genetic operator overhead

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

1. In the PSO velocity update equation, what does the cognitive component represent?

The cognitive term c₁·r₁·(pbest - x) reflects individual memory and learning.

2. Why is the inertia weight w often decreased linearly from 0.9 to 0.4 over the course of PSO iterations?

High inertia maintains large step sizes early on, while lower inertia helps the swarm settle and fine-tune into local minima.

3. Which fundamental mechanism is present in PSO but completely absent in standard Genetic Algorithms?

GA chromosomes do not retain memory of where they were in prior generations; PSO particles track their own historical best.

Recall without looking

Write the velocity and position update equations for PSO.

v_{id}^{(t+1)} = w · v_{id}^{(t)} + c₁ · r₁ · (pbest_{id} − x_{id}^{(t)}) + c₂ · r₂ · (gbest_d − x_{id}^{(t)}).
x_{id}^{(t+1)} = x_{id}^{(t)} + v_{id}^{(t+1)}.

What do parameters w, c₁, and c₂ signify in PSO?

w = inertia weight (momentum/exploration-exploitation balance). c₁ = cognitive factor (pull toward pbest). c₂ = social factor (pull toward gbest). Typically c₁ ≈ c₂ ≈ 2.0.

Name three key differences between PSO and GA.

1. PSO uses velocity/position math; GA uses crossover/mutation. 2. PSO particles retain personal memory (pbest); GA does not. 3. PSO operates naturally on real/continuous spaces; GA traditionally uses discrete/binary chromosomes.

Medical Pioneers: Caraka, Suśruta & Jīvaka

Internal medicine in the Caraka Saṃhitā, plastic surgery and instruments in the Suśruta Saṃhitā, and neurosurgery by Jīvaka.

1 · Caraka: The Father of Internal Medicine (Kāyacikitsā)

Sage Caraka (c. 300 BCE – 200 CE) compiled and redacted the monumental medical treatise Caraka Saṃhitā, the foundational authority on general and internal medicine in Āyurveda.

Core Diagnostic & Physiological Principles
  • Tridoṣa Theory: Human health represents dynamic equilibrium among three biological humors:
    • Vāta (Ether + Air): Governs all movement, respiration, nerve impulses, and circulation.
    • Pitta (Fire + Water): Governs metabolic transformation, digestion, body temperature, and intellect.
    • Kapha (Water + Earth): Governs structural stability, lubrication, cohesion, and tissue immunity.
    Disease (*Roga*) is defined as an imbalance (*Doṣa-vaiṣamya*); health (*Svāsthya*) is equilibrium.
  • Saptadhātu (Seven Body Tissues): The metabolic cascade where food converts sequentially into: Rasa (plasma) → Rakta (blood) → Māṃsa (muscle) → Meda (fat) → Asthi (bone) → Majjā (bone marrow) → Śukra (reproductive fluid) → yielding the supreme immune essence, Ojas.
  • Medical Ethics (Śiṣyopanayana): In the *Vimānasthāna*, Caraka outlines an ethical oath that every physician must take before practicing: to dedicate themselves selflessly to the cure of patients, never abandon the sick, treat poor patients free of charge, and strictly maintain medical confidentiality.

2 · Suśruta: The Father of Surgery & Plastic Surgery

Practicing in Varanasi (c. 800–600 BCE), Sage Suśruta was a legendary genius recognized worldwide as the Father of Surgery and the pioneer of Rhinoplasty (Plastic Surgery).

Surgical BreakthroughHistorical Details from Suśruta Saṃhitā
Rhinoplasty (Nāsā-sandhāna) Reconstructed severed noses (a common punishment for adultery in antiquity) using a living pedicled skin flap taken from the forehead or cheek. Outlined step-by-step: cutting the flap, scarifying the nose stump, stitching with precision, inserting hollow castor-oil stems for breathing, and applying healing medicinal pastes. Adopted in Europe 2,400 years later as the "Indian Method of Rhinoplasty".
Cataract Couching Extracapsular cataract surgery performed using a curved needle (*Yavamukhi Śalākā*) to displace the opaque lens downward into the vitreous cavity, restoring functional vision.
121 Surgical Instruments Detailed 101 blunt instruments (Yantras: forceps, speculums, probes modeled after the jaws and beaks of animals and birds) and 20 sharp instruments (Śastras: scalpels, lancets, trocars, bone saws) made of high-quality tempered steel.
Eight Surgical Operations (Aṣṭavidha Śastrakarma) Chedana (excision), Bhedana (incision), Lekhana (scraping), Vedhana (puncturing), Eṣaṇa (probing), Āharaṇa (extraction of foreign bodies/kidney stones), Visrāvaṇa (drainage of fluids/abscesses), Sīvana (suturing using vegetal fibers, horsehair, or giant black ant mandibles).
Anatomical Dissection in Water Prescribed placing dead bodies in bamboo cages inside flowing stream water for seven days until tissues soften, allowing layers of skin, fascia, blood vessels, and internal organs to be peeled away and studied with fine brushes.

3 · Jīvaka Komārabhacca: The Royal Physician & Neurosurgeon

Living around 500 BCE during the time of Gautama Buddha, Jīvaka was born in Rajgir (Magadha), raised by Prince Abhaya, and traveled to Takṣaśilā University where he studied Āyurveda for seven years under master teacher Ātreya.

Jīvaka's Famous Graduation Test & Clinical Miracles
  • The Graduation Examination: Ātreya commanded Jīvaka to search within a radius of one yojana (approx. 8–10 miles) around Takṣaśilā and bring back a plant that had zero medicinal value. Jīvaka returned empty-handed after days of searching, explaining: "Master, I could not find a single plant on this earth that does not possess medicinal efficacy." Ātreya declared him fully graduated.
  • Cranial Trepanation (Brain Surgery): Treated a wealthy merchant in Rajgir who had suffered from unbearable head migraines for years. Jīvaka tied the patient to a bed, opened the skull bone (trepanation), removed two intracranial parasites, stitched the cranial tissues, and cured the patient completely.
  • Abdominal Laparotomy: Successfully operated on a patient suffering from twisted intestines by making an incision, untangling the obstructed bowels, stitching the internal walls, and restoring full intestinal digestion.
  • Physician to Buddha & King Bimbisāra: Cured King Bimbisāra of a severe fistula using an herbal ointment, and was appointed royal physician to attend to Gautama Buddha and the Buddhist Sangha. Influenced King Ajātaśatru to embrace Buddhism.

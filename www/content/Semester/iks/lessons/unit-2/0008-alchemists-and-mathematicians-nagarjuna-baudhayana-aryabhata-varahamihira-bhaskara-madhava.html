Alchemists & Mathematicians: Nāgārjuna, Bodhāyana, Āryabhaṭa, Varāhamihira, Bhāskara & Mādhava

Zinc metallurgy, the Bodhāyana-Pythagoras theorem, spherical rotating Earth, differential calculus precursors, and the Kerala School of infinite series.

1 · Nāgārjuna: Buddhist Philosopher & Father of Alchemy (Rasaśāstra)

Sage Nāgārjuna (c. 2nd–3rd century CE) held an extraordinary double legacy: he founded the Mādhyamaka school of Buddhist philosophy (the doctrine of *Śūnyatā* / Interdependent Emptiness) AND pioneered Indian metallurgical chemistry (Rasaśāstra) in his treatise Rasaratnākara.

  • Zinc Distillation Metallurgy: Pure zinc has a boiling point (907°C) lower than the temperature required to reduce zinc ore (1000°C), meaning it instantly vaporizes upon smelting. Nāgārjuna invented downward distillation retorts (*Koshthi apparatus*) where vaporized zinc was condensed upside down over cooling water pots—pioneered in Zawar, Rajasthan centuries before William Champion patented it in England in 1738.
  • Redaction of Suśruta Saṃhitā: Redacted the ancient surgical text, adding the comprehensive Uttaratantra covering ophthalmology, ENT diseases, and pediatrics.

2 · Bodhāyana: The First Geometer (c. 800 BCE)

Author of the Bodhāyana Śulba Sūtras, the earliest geometry manual composed for designing precise fire altars (*Yajña-vedis*):

The Bodhāyana-Pythagoras Theorem (Śulba Sūtra 1.48)

"Dīrghacaturasyākṣṇayā rajjuḥ pārśvamānī tiryaṅmānī ca yatpṛthagbhūte kurutastadubhayaṃ karoti ||"
Translation: "The diagonal cord of a rectangle produces both areas which its length and breadth produce separately."
This explicit geometric statement was documented centuries before Pythagoras was born!

Astonishing Approximation of the Square Root of Two (√2)

Śulba Sūtra 1.61 gives the formula for the diagonal of a unit square:
√2 ≈ 1 + 1/3 + 1/(3 × 4) - 1/(3 × 4 × 34) = 577 / 408 ≈ 1.4142156
Accurate to five decimal places! (True value: 1.41421356).

3 · Āryabhaṭa: The Colossus of Astronomy & Trigonometry (476–550 CE)

At the youthful age of 23 in Kusumapura (Pataliputra/Patna), Āryabhaṭa composed the Āryabhaṭīya, revolutionizing science:

  • Earth's Spherical Shape & Axial Rotation: "Just as a passenger riding in a moving boat sees stationary trees on the bank moving backward, so observers on Earth see the stationary stars moving westward." First astronomer to declare that Earth is spherical and rotates daily on its axis!
  • True Cause of Eclipses: Replaced demon myths with scientific physics: the Moon and planets shine by reflected sunlight. Lunar eclipses occur when Earth's shadow falls on the Moon; solar eclipses occur when the Moon casts its shadow upon Earth.
  • Approximation of Pi (π): "Add 4 to 100, multiply by 8, add 62,000. For a diameter of 20,000, the circumference is 62,832." → π ≈ 62832 / 20000 = 3.1416. Explicitly used the word "Āsanna" (approximated), recognizing that π is incommensurable (irrational)!
  • Trigonometry: Invented trigonometric sine (*Jardha* → *Jya*) and cosine (*Kotijya*), constructing sine difference tables.

4 · Varāhamihira: The Universal Polymath (505–587 CE)

Astronomer at the royal court of Ujjain; authored the Pañcasiddhāntikā (summarizing five ancient astronomical schools) and the encyclopedic Bṛhat Saṃhitā (covering planetary motions, meteorology, cloud formations, gemology, architecture, and subterranean hydrology using termite mounds and indicator plants).

5 · Bhāskarācārya (Bhāskara II): Siddhānta Śiromaṇi (1114–1185 CE)

Director of the astronomical observatory in Ujjain; author of the immortal Siddhānta Śiromaṇi (comprising Līlāvatī, Bījagaṇita, Grahagaṇita, and Golādhyāya):

Mathematical DiscoveryBhāskara II's Contribution
Precursor to Differential Calculus Formulated the concept of instantaneous velocity (Tātkālikī-gati) 500 years before Newton and Leibniz: d(sin θ) = cos θ dθ. Discovered that when a variable reaches its maximum value, its differential rate of change vanishes.
The Infinite Nature of Division by Zero In Bījagaṇita, stated that a finite quantity divided by zero results in an infinite quantity (Khahara): a / 0 = ∞, explaining that just as infinite God is unaffected when universes enter or leave Him, infinite quantity is unchanged by addition or subtraction.
The Cakraavāla Cyclic Method Indeterminate quadratic equations (Pell's equation: Nx2 + 1 = y2). Solved centuries before Euler and Lagrange. Mathematician C.J. Selenius called it "the peak of human mathematical subtlety prior to modern times."

6 · Mādhavācārya: The Kerala School of Infinite Series (1340–1425 CE)

The True Founder of Mathematical Analysis

Hailing from Sangamagrama (near Irinjalakuda, Kerala), Mādhava founded the famous Kerala School of Astronomy and Mathematics. He discovered infinite power series expansions for π, sine, cosine, and arctangent 250 to 300 years before European mathematicians:

  • Mādhava-Leibniz Series for π: π / 4 = 1 - 1/3 + 1/5 - 1/7 + 1/9 - ... (attributed to Leibniz in 1673; discovered by Madhava in 1400!).
  • Mādhava-Gregory Series for Arctangent: arctan(x) = x - x3/3 + x5/5 - x7/7 + ... (attributed to James Gregory in 1667).
  • Mādhava Series for Sine and Cosine (attributed to Isaac Newton in 1669).
  • Invented remainder error-correction terms to accelerate the convergence of infinite series.

His school's discoveries were documented in Jyeṣṭhadeva's Yuktibhāṣā (c. 1530 CE)—the world's first comprehensive calculus textbook written in Malayalam prose!

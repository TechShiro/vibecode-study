The Main Schools of Bhāratīya Philosophy (6 + 3)

Darśana as a worldview: the six orthodox (Āstika) and three heterodox (Nāstika) systems, their founders, and epistemology.

1 · The Concept of Darśana

The English word "philosophy" means love of wisdom (an intellectual hobby). In India, philosophy is called Darśana, derived from the Sanskrit verbal root dṛś (to see). A Darśana is not mere speculative logic; it is a direct perception or experiential vision of reality that transforms human life and removes suffering.

All Bhāratīya philosophical inquiry examines the relationship between three entities:

  • Jīva: The individual living being / conscious soul.
  • Jagat: The objective phenomenal universe / matter.
  • Īśvara: The supreme cosmic consciousness / divine principle.

2 · The Classification: Āstika vs Nāstika

Crucial Exam Distinction

In Indian philosophy, Āstika does NOT mean "theist" (believer in God), and Nāstika does NOT mean "atheist"!

  • Āstika (Orthodox): Schools that accept the epistemic authority of the Vedas (even if they do not require a creator God, e.g. early Sāṃkhya and Mīmāṃsā).
  • Nāstika (Heterodox): Schools that do NOT accept the authority of the Vedas (Cārvāka, Buddhism, Jainism).

3 · The Six Āstika Darśanas (Ṣaḍ-Darśana)

The six orthodox systems are traditionally paired based on complementary metaphysics and epistemology:

DarśanaFounder / SagePrimary TextCore Philosophical Concept
Nyāya Sage Gautama (Akṣapāda) Nyāya Sūtras Logic & Epistemology. Establishes the 4 valid means of knowledge (Pramāṇas): Pratyakṣa (perception), Anumāna (inference), Upamāna (comparison), Śabda (testimony). Uses a 5-step syllogism to prove truths.
Vaiśeṣika Sage Kaṇāda (Ulūka) Vaiśeṣika Sūtras Physics & Atomism. Postulates that the physical universe is composed of indivisible, eternal atoms (Paramāṇu). Classifies reality into 7 categories of existence (Padārthas).
Sāṃkhya Sage Kapila Sāṃkhya Sūtras Metaphysical Dualism. The cosmos evolves through the interaction of Puruṣa (pure unattached consciousness) and Prakṛti (dynamic primordial matter) governed by 3 Guṇas: Sattva, Rajas, Tamas.
Yoga Sage Patañjali Yoga Sūtras Practical Psychology & Mind Discipline. Defines Yoga as "Citta-vṛtti-nirodha" (stilling the fluctuations of consciousness). Teaches the Aṣṭāṅga Yoga (8 Limbs: Yama, Niyama, Āsana, Prāṇāyāma, Pratyāhāra, Dhāraṇā, Dhyāna, Samādhi).
Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā) Sage Jaimini Mīmāṃsā Sūtras Vedic Hermeneutics & Action. Focuses on the philosophy of Dharma, sacrificial duties, the eternal nature of sound (*Śabda*), and righteous social duty.
Vedānta (Uttara Mīmāṃsā) Sage Bādarāyaṇa Brahma Sūtras Philosophy of Ultimate Reality. Explores the identity of Brahman and Ātman. Developed into Advaita (Non-dualism of Śaṅkara), Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja), and Dvaita (Madhva).

4 · The Three Nāstika Darśanas

1. Cārvāka (Lokāyata)

Founder: Bṛhaspati (attributed).
Philosophy: Radical materialism and empiricism. Rejects rebirth, karma, heaven, and soul. Accepts ONLY direct sensory perception (Pratyakṣa) as valid knowledge. "Live joyfully while you live; eat ghee even if you must borrow money!"

2. Buddhism (Bauddha)

Founder: Gautama Buddha.
Philosophy: Four Noble Truths (Suffering, Cause, Cessation, Path). The Noble Eightfold Path. Rejects permanent soul (Anattā); everything is impermanent (Anicca) and dependent on causes (Pratītyasamutpāda).

3. Jainism (Jaina)

Founders: 24 Tīrthaṅkaras (Mahāvīra).
Philosophy: Ahiṃsā (supreme non-violence). Anekāntavāda (multi-sided nature of truth: reality is too complex for any single dogmatic perspective) and Syādvāda (the doctrine of conditional predication).

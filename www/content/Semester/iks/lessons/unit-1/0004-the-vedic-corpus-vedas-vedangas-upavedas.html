The Vedic Corpus: Vedas, Vedāṅgas & Upavedas

The fourfold division of the Vedas, their four textual layers, the six auxiliary sciences, and applied knowledge.

1 · The Four Vedas & Their Organisation

Originally, the Vedic knowledge was an immense single continuum known as Trayī Vidyā (the threefold knowledge of Ṛk, Yajus, and Sāma). Sage Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana, recognizing that human memory and lifespan would decline in Kali Yuga, systematically edited and divided the corpus into four major Vedas, earning the immortal title Veda Vyāsa (the Compiler/Organizer of the Vedas).

To guarantee uninterrupted transmission, Vyasa appointed four principal disciples to head each lineage:

VedaDesignated DiscipleChief Priest (Ṛtvik)Core Nature & Content
Ṛgveda Paila Hotā (invoker) The oldest and largest repository. Contains 10,552 verses across 10 Maṇḍalas and 1,028 Sūktas. Hymns celebrating natural, cosmic, and spiritual forces (Agni, Indra, Varuṇa, Saraswatī, Uṣas).
Yajurveda Vaiśampāyana Adhvaryu (performer) The sacrificial and procedural formula veda. Divided into Śukla (White) (pure mantras preserved by Yājñavalkya) and Kṛṣṇa (Black) (mantras mixed with prose commentaries). Contains earliest geometric altar designs.
Sāmaveda Jaimini Udgātā (singer) The musical veda. Verses taken largely from Ṛgveda, set to specific melodic chanting scales (Svara). The root and origin of Indian classical music.
Atharvaveda Sumantu Brahmā (supervisor) The veda of everyday life, applied sciences, statecraft, botany, toxicology, and medicine. Contains foundational seeds of Āyurveda and metallurgical metallurgy.

2 · The Four Layers of Vedic Literature

Each of the four Vedas is structured into four sequential, evolutionary layers:

1. Saṃhitās (The Mantras)

The core poetic collections of prayers, invocations, and cosmic insights received in deep meditation by ancient Ṛṣis.

2. Brāhmaṇas (The Procedures)

Prose treatises explaining the practical and symbolic procedures of rituals, altar constructions, and historical legends.

3. Āraṇyakas (Forest Treatises)

Meditative and philosophical reflections designed for hermits dwelling in forests, transitioning from external rituals to internal contemplative states.

4. Upaniṣads (Vedānta)

The philosophical climax and culmination of the Vedas. Investigates ultimate reality (Brahman), the true Self (Ātman), and the nature of the cosmos.

3 · The Six Vedāṅgas (Limbs of the Veda)

To accurately read, pronounce, understand, and apply the Vedas, six auxiliary scientific disciplines were developed, conceptualized as parts of the personified Veda-Puruṣa:

4 · The Four Upavedas (Applied Sciences)

  • Āyurveda (associated with Ṛgveda or Atharvaveda): Science of longevity, holistic medicine, physiology, pathology, and surgery.
  • Dhanurveda (associated with Yajurveda): Military sciences, weaponry, archery, defensive martial arts, and strategic warfare.
  • Gāndharvaveda (associated with Sāmaveda): Performing arts, classical music, dance, theater, and vocal resonance.
  • Sthāpatyaveda / Arthaśāstra (associated with Atharvaveda): Architecture, civil engineering, temple town planning, metallurgy, and state economy.

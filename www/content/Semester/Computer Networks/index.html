How the videos are organised

Gate Smashers as your spine, Neso as your fallback. Do not watch two playlists in parallel — pick one per topic, and switch only when a specific concept refuses to land. Every lesson has a Watch box naming the exact videos for that topic, so you never need to hunt through a 106-video playlist for the right one.

How to attack this subject

  1. Subnetting is the single highest marks-per-hour topic. Lesson 22. Do twenty problems, not two — it is a motor skill, and reading about it does not build it.
  2. The numericals method handbook gives the recognition index and the step-by-step procedure for all 32 numerical types — the lessons have the worked examples, that page has the method to apply to an unseen question.
  3. Units II, III and IV carry the numericals — Nyquist/Shannon, CRC, Hamming, sliding-window efficiency, ALOHA, subnetting, fragmentation, congestion windows, RTO, RSA. Numericals are the most reliable marks in the paper because they are objectively right or wrong.
  4. Units I and V are table-and-diagram units. OSI vs TCP/IP, TCP vs UDP, DNS resolution, DORA, the SSL handshake. Learn to draw each in under three minutes.
  5. Six diagrams are worth practising until they are automatic: the OSI stack, the Ethernet frame, the IPv4 header, the TCP three-way handshake, the congestion-window sawtooth, and DNS resolution.
  6. Print the five exam sheets. Each ends with a ten-point final scan written for the last ten minutes before you go in.

The syllabus is complete. The one thing still worth asking for is a drill sheet — subnetting, CRC and Hamming, with a separate answer key. Those three convert into marks through practice, not reading. Past papers, if you can get them, would sharpen everything else.

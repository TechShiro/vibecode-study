Analog and Digital Data and Signals

The physical layer starts here: what data and signals are, the three attributes of a sine wave, the time and frequency domains, bandwidth, and the three transmission impairments. Mostly definitions and small numericals — cheap marks if you learn the vocabulary precisely.

Exam strategy

This topic is asked in small pieces, not one big question: "differentiate analog and digital signals" (3 marks), "define bandwidth of a composite signal" (2 marks), "explain transmission impairments" (5 marks), plus a one-line dB or SNR calculation. Learn the definitions and units exactly — that is where the marks are, not in explanation.

Data vs signals

Data is the information we want to send. Signals are the electromagnetic representation of that data as it travels on the medium. Both can be analog or digital, and the two choices are independent.

BasisAnalogDigital
ValuesContinuous — infinitely many values in a rangeDiscrete — a limited, finite set of values
WaveformSmooth, continuously varying curveAbrupt, stepped — a square wave
Example of dataThe human voice, a temperature readingData stored in computer memory as 0s and 1s
Described byAmplitude, frequency, phaseBit rate, bit interval, bit length
BandwidthFinite — a fixed range of frequenciesInfinite in theory — a square wave is a sum of infinitely many sine waves
Noise immunityPoor — noise adds directly to the signalGood — the receiver only has to decide "high or low"

Periodic and non-periodic signals

  • A periodic signal completes a pattern within a measurable time frame (a period) and repeats that pattern over identical subsequent periods.
  • A non-periodic (aperiodic) signal changes without exhibiting any repeating pattern.

The rule worth remembering: in data communication we commonly use periodic analog signals and non-periodic digital signals.

The sine wave and its three attributes

The sine wave is the most fundamental form of a periodic analog signal. It is completely described by three characteristics — and "explain the characteristics of a sine wave" is a standard 3-mark question.

AttributeMeaningUnit
AmplitudeThe value of the signal at any point on the wave — its height. Equal to the absolute value of its highest intensity.Volts, amperes or watts
Frequency (f)The number of periods completed in one second — the rate of change with respect to time.Hertz (Hz)
PhaseThe position of the waveform relative to time zero — how far the wave is shifted forward or backward along the time axis.Degrees or radians
Period and frequency are inverses f = 1 / T    and    T = 1 / f
where T is the period in seconds and f the frequency in hertz
Wavelength λ = c / f    (propagation speed ÷ frequency)
Two one-liners that earn marks

Frequency is the rate of change with respect to time. So: a signal that does not change at all has a frequency of zero; a signal that changes instantaneously has a frequency of infinity. Examiners like this pair because it shows you understand frequency rather than just quoting a unit.

Worked numerical — period and frequency

Q. A sine wave has a period of 100 ms. What is its frequency in kilohertz?

T = 100 ms = 100 × 10−3 s = 10−1 s

f = 1/T = 1 / 10−1 = 10 Hz = 10 × 10−3 kHz = 0.01 kHz

Sanity check: a long period means a low frequency. 100 ms is a tenth of a second, so ten cycles fit in a second. ✓

Time domain and frequency domain

Two ways to draw the same signal, and a favourite short question.

Time-domain plotFrequency-domain plot
ShowsAmplitude against timeAmplitude against frequency

RevealsHow the signal changes over timeThe frequency components and their peak amplitudes

The frequency domain is used because it is far more compact and useful when a signal is made of many sine waves: a signal of three components is three spikes, whereas its time-domain plot is one complicated curve.

Composite signals and bandwidth

A single-frequency sine wave is useless for data communication — it carries no information because nothing about it changes. Real signals are composite: made of many simple sine waves.

Fourier analysis is the result that makes this precise: any composite signal can be decomposed into a series of simple sine waves with different frequencies, amplitudes and phases. Two consequences, both examinable:

  • If the composite signal is periodic, its decomposition is a set of signals with discrete frequencies — the frequency domain shows separate spikes.
  • If it is non-periodic, the decomposition is a continuous combination of infinitely many frequencies — the frequency domain shows a continuous curve.
Bandwidth of a composite signal Bandwidth = fhighest − flowest

Bandwidth of a composite signal is the difference between the highest and lowest frequencies it contains. Note this is bandwidth measured in hertz — the analog sense — and is a different quantity from bandwidth in bits per second, which is the digital sense.

Worked numerical — bandwidth

Q. A periodic signal contains frequencies from 1000 Hz to 5000 Hz. Find its bandwidth. If the lowest frequency is then shifted to 100 Hz with the bandwidth unchanged, what is the new highest frequency?

Bandwidth = 5000 − 1000 = 4000 Hz

New highest = lowest + bandwidth = 100 + 4000 = 4100 Hz

Sanity check: bandwidth is a width, so sliding the whole band up or down does not change it. ✓

Digital signals: bit rate and bit length

Digital signal terms Bit rate = number of bits sent per second (bps)
Bit interval = time required to send one bit = 1 / bit rate
Bit length = propagation speed × bit interval

A digital signal with L levels carries log2L bits per level. So a signal with 4 levels carries 2 bits per level; with 8 levels, 3 bits.

TransmissionWhat it isNeeds
BasebandSending a digital signal over a channel without changing it to an analog signalA low-pass channel — bandwidth starting at zero
Broadband (modulation)Changing the digital signal to an analog signal for transmissionA bandpass channel — bandwidth not starting at zero

Transmission impairment

Signals travel through media, and media are imperfect. Three impairments — a reliable 5-mark question, one paragraph each with a sketch.

ImpairmentWhat happensFixed by
AttenuationLoss of energy — the signal gets weaker as it travels, because it must overcome the resistance of the medium.Amplifiers (analog) or repeaters (digital), which regenerate rather than merely boost
DistortionThe signal changes its form or shape. Happens in composite signals: each component has a different propagation speed through the medium, so they arrive with different delays and the recombined shape differs from the original.Equalisation; limiting bandwidth
NoiseUnwanted energy is added to the signal. Types: thermal noise (random electron motion), induced noise (from motors and appliances), crosstalk (one wire affecting another), impulse noise (a spike from lightning or a power line).Shielding, twisting the pair, error detection and correction

Decibels and signal-to-noise ratio

Decibel dB = 10 log10 (P2 / P1)

A negative dB means the signal was attenuated (P2 < P1); a positive dB means it was amplified. Decibels are used because they are additive: the total effect of a chain of links is the sum of their individual dB values, not the product of their ratios.

Signal-to-noise ratio SNR = average signal power ÷ average noise power
SNRdB = 10 log10 SNR

A high SNR means the signal is less corrupted by noise, and so a higher data rate is possible. SNR is the input to Shannon's capacity formula in the next lesson — that is where it earns its marks.

Worked numerical — attenuation in dB

Q. A signal travels through a link and its power is reduced to one half. Express the attenuation in decibels.

P2 = 0.5 P1, so P2/P1 = 0.5

dB = 10 log10(0.5) = 10 × (−0.3) = −3 dB

The fact worth memorising: halving the power is exactly −3 dB. Doubling it is +3 dB. Most dB questions in this syllabus reduce to that one relationship.

Digital-to-Analog Conversion (Modulation)

Digital-to-analog conversion is the process of translating digital binary data (a stream of 1s and 0s) into an analog signal for transmission over a bandpass medium. The sender modulates a high-frequency sine wave — called the carrier signal — by altering one or more of its fundamental characteristics (amplitude, frequency, or phase) according to the data bits.

Baud Rate vs Bit Rate · The Core Formula $$\text{Bit Rate } N = \text{Baud Rate } S \times r = S \times \log_2(L)$$
$$\text{Baud Rate } S = \frac{N}{r} = \frac{N}{\log_2(L)} \text{ baud (signal elements per second)}$$
$$\text{Minimum Bandwidth } B = (1 + d) \times S = (1 + d) \times \frac{N}{r}$$ where $L$ is the number of distinct signal element levels, $r$ is the number of data bits carried per signal element ($r = \log_2 L$), and $d$ is a transmission factor related to filtering ($0 \le d \le 1$, typically $d = 0$ for ideal minimum bandwidth).
Bit rate vs Baud rate — The one-line examiner question

Bit rate ($N$) is the number of bits transmitted per second (bps). Baud rate ($S$) is the number of signal elements (symbols) transmitted per second (baud). In digital data transmission, baud rate is always less than or equal to bit rate: $S \le N$. Baud rate determines the required bandwidth of the channel, not bit rate.

Analog-to-Analog Conversion

Analog-to-analog conversion (analog modulation) is the representation of low-frequency analog information (such as voice, audio, or analog sensor output) using a high-frequency analog carrier signal. It is necessary when the transmission medium is bandpass by nature (e.g., free space / radio frequency spectrum), where low-frequency baseband signals cannot radiate efficiently because required antenna length $L \approx \lambda/4 = c / (4f)$ would be impractically large (several kilometres for audio frequencies!).

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

1. A signal whose value never changes has a frequency of:

Frequency measures change per unit time. No change means zero frequency; instantaneous change means infinite frequency. This pair is the standard way examiners test whether you understand the definition.

2. In the frequency domain, a single simple sine wave is drawn as:

One frequency, one spike. The whole point of the frequency-domain plot is that a composite of three sine waves becomes three spikes rather than one messy curve.

3. A composite signal contains frequencies from 200 Hz to 900 Hz. Its bandwidth is:

Bandwidth is a width: highest minus lowest. Note this is bandwidth in hertz (analog); bandwidth in bits per second is a different quantity with the same name.

4. Different frequency components of a composite signal arrive at different times, changing the received shape. This is:

Attenuation weakens; noise adds; distortion reshapes. Distortion specifically affects composite signals, because it arises from components propagating at different speeds.

5. Halving the power of a signal corresponds to an attenuation of:

10 log₁₀(0.5) = 10 × (−0.3) = −3 dB. Memorise this one value: halving is −3 dB, doubling is +3 dB, and most dB questions in this syllabus are built from it.

Recall without looking

Give four differences between analog and digital signals.

Analog has continuous values, a smooth waveform, is described by amplitude/frequency/phase, and has finite bandwidth. Digital has discrete values, a stepped square waveform, is described by bit rate/bit interval/bit length, and has theoretically infinite bandwidth. Digital also has far better noise immunity.

Name and define the three characteristics of a sine wave.

Amplitude — the value (height) of the signal at any point, in volts. Frequency — the number of periods per second, in hertz; the rate of change with respect to time. Phase — the position of the waveform relative to time zero, in degrees.

State Fourier analysis and its two consequences.

Any composite signal can be decomposed into a series of simple sine waves with different frequencies, amplitudes and phases. If the composite signal is periodic, the decomposition gives discrete frequencies; if non-periodic, a continuous combination of infinitely many frequencies.

Define bandwidth of a composite signal.

The difference between the highest and lowest frequencies contained in the signal, measured in hertz.

Name the three transmission impairments and one cause of each.

Attenuation — loss of energy overcoming the resistance of the medium. Distortion — components travelling at different speeds so the shape changes. Noise — unwanted energy added; thermal, induced, crosstalk, impulse.

Why are decibels used rather than plain power ratios?

Because dB values are additive: the effect of a chain of links is the sum of their dB values. A negative dB means attenuation, a positive dB means amplification.

Compare ASK, FSK, PSK, and QAM on carrier characteristics and noise immunity.

ASK changes amplitude (fixed frequency and phase; simplest but most vulnerable to noise). FSK changes frequency (fixed amplitude and phase; high noise immunity but requires larger bandwidth). PSK changes phase (fixed amplitude and frequency; excellent noise immunity and bandwidth efficiency; QPSK carries 2 bits/baud). QAM modulates both amplitude and phase simultaneously, providing the highest bandwidth efficiency (e.g., 16-QAM sends 4 bits/baud, 64-QAM sends 6 bits/baud).

What is Carson's rule for FM bandwidth?

B_FM = 2(1 + β)B, where β is the modulation index (Δf / B) and B is the audio bandwidth. It accounts for the carrier plus all significant sideband pairs, giving roughly 10 times the baseband bandwidth (typically 200 kHz in FM broadcast vs 10 kHz in AM).

Difference between baseband and broadband transmission?

Baseband sends the digital signal unchanged and needs a low-pass channel (bandwidth starting at zero). Broadband converts the digital signal to analog by modulation and uses a bandpass channel (bandwidth not starting at zero).

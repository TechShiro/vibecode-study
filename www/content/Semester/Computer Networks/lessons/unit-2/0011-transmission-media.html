Transmission Media

Guided and unguided media — twisted pair, coaxial, fibre optic, radio, microwave and infrared. A descriptive topic with a fixed structure: definition, construction, advantages, disadvantages, applications. Learn the structure and every sub-question answers itself.

Exam strategy

Asked as "Explain guided transmission media with diagrams" (10 marks) or "Compare twisted pair, coaxial and fibre optic" (5 marks, table). For the long version, use the same five headings for each medium — construction, how it carries the signal, advantages, disadvantages, applications — and draw the cross-section of each cable. Consistency of structure reads as command of the material and is quick to write.

Guided media

1. Twisted-pair cable

Construction: two conductors, each with its own plastic insulation, twisted together. One wire carries the signal, the other is used as a ground reference.

Why the wires are twisted — the exam's favourite sub-question

If the two wires were parallel, the wire nearer to a noise source would receive more interference, and the resulting difference between the two wires would be read as signal. By twisting them, each wire is closer to the noise source for half the twists and further for the other half, so both receive the same interference and the unwanted signals cancel at the receiver. Write that whole explanation; "to reduce noise" alone is a partial answer.

Advantages: cheapest medium, easy to install, adequate for short distances.

2. Coaxial cable

Construction, from the inside out: a central copper core conductor → an insulating sheath → an outer conductor of metal foil or braid, which acts both as the second conductor and as a shield → an outer plastic jacket. The two conductors share the same axis — hence "co-axial".

  • Carries higher frequency ranges than twisted pair, because of the concentric construction and the shielding.
  • Standards: RG-59 (75 Ω, cable TV), RG-58 (50 Ω, thin Ethernet / 10BASE2), RG-11 (50 Ω, thick Ethernet / 10BASE5).
  • Connector: BNC connector, BNC T-connector (to tap a station onto the cable), and BNC terminator (to prevent signal reflection at the cable ends).
  • Applications: Cable television distribution, classic bus-topology Ethernet, long-distance analog trunk lines.

3. Fibre-optic cable

Construction: a glass or plastic core surrounded by a cladding of less dense glass or plastic, wrapped in a protective jacket. It carries signals as pulses of light rather than electrical current.

The physics, in the two sentences the exam wants

Fibre optics works by total internal reflection. The core has a higher refractive index (denser) than the cladding; when light strikes the boundary at an angle greater than the critical angle, it is reflected entirely back into the core rather than refracting out, so it propagates along the fibre by repeated reflection. Get the density relationship the right way round — core denser than cladding — because it is the half students most often reverse.

Unguided media

Signals are broadcast through free space and are available to anyone with a device able to receive them. Propagation happens three ways: ground propagation (low frequency, follows the curvature of the earth), sky propagation (higher frequency, reflected off the ionosphere), and line-of-sight propagation (very high frequency, straight lines between antennas).

Satellite communication is the large-scale microwave case: a signal is sent up to a satellite (uplink) and retransmitted back down over a wide area (downlink). Orbits: GEO (35,786 km, appears stationary, three cover the earth, but ~250 ms delay), MEO (GPS), LEO (low delay, many satellites needed).

The infrared point examiners like

Infrared's inability to penetrate walls is presented as an advantage, not a limitation: it means a short-range infrared system in one room cannot be affected by, or interfere with, a system in an adjacent room. It also makes eavesdropping from outside the room impossible.

Check yourself

Retrieval practice

1. The wires in a twisted-pair cable are twisted in order to:

With parallel wires the nearer one picks up more interference and the difference is misread as signal. Twisting equalises the exposure so the unwanted signals cancel at the receiver. That mechanism, not just "to reduce noise", is what the mark is for.

2. In an optical fibre, total internal reflection occurs because:

Core denser (higher refractive index) than cladding is the condition for total internal reflection. Reversing the two is the most common error on this question, and it makes the physics impossible.

3. Which fibre mode uses a laser source and supports the longest distances?

Single-mode's narrow core forces all light onto nearly identical paths, so distortion is minimal and the reach is greatest. Multimode uses an LED and suffers distortion from differing path lengths — graded-index reduces that, but does not eliminate it.

4. Which is a genuine disadvantage of fibre-optic cable?

Fibre's real drawbacks are unidirectionality, cost, fragility and the expertise needed to install it. Its immunity to EMI, low attenuation and huge bandwidth are its advantages — the other three options invert them.

5. Microwave transmission differs from radio wave transmission mainly in that microwaves:

Radio waves are omnidirectional and penetrate walls; microwaves are focused by a dish, need aligned antennas, and cannot pass through walls. Satellite links are the largest-scale microwave application, so the last option is doubly wrong.

Recall without looking

Define guided and unguided media.

Guided media provide a physical conduit from one device to another; the signal is directed and contained by the physical limits of the medium (twisted pair, coaxial, fibre). Unguided media transport electromagnetic waves without a physical conductor, through free space (radio, microwave, infrared).

Why are the wires in a twisted pair twisted?

So that each wire is closer to a noise source for half the twists and further for the other half. Both wires then receive the same interference, and the unwanted signals cancel at the receiver instead of appearing as a difference that is read as signal.

Describe the construction of coaxial cable.

A central copper core conductor, an insulating sheath, an outer conductor of metal foil or braid acting as the second conductor and as a shield, and an outer plastic jacket. Both conductors share the same axis. Connector: BNC.

Explain how a fibre-optic cable carries a signal.

By total internal reflection. The core has a higher refractive index (is denser) than the cladding; light striking the boundary at an angle greater than the critical angle is reflected entirely back into the core and propagates by repeated reflection.

Name the three fibre modes and one distinguishing feature of each.

Multimode step-index — abrupt density change at the boundary, many path lengths, most distortion. Multimode graded-index — density decreases gradually from the centre, bending beams into curves, less distortion. Single-mode — very narrow core, near-horizontal paths, laser source, longest distance.

Give four advantages and three disadvantages of fibre.

Advantages: enormous bandwidth, low attenuation, immunity to electromagnetic interference, light weight and corrosion resistance, and high security. Disadvantages: installation and maintenance need expertise, it is unidirectional so full-duplex needs two fibres, higher cost, and it is fragile.

Compare radio, microwave and infrared on directionality and wall penetration.

Radio (3 kHz–1 GHz): omnidirectional, penetrates walls, no line of sight needed. Microwave (1–300 GHz): unidirectional, cannot penetrate walls, needs line of sight. Infrared (300 GHz–400 THz): unidirectional, cannot penetrate walls — which prevents interference between adjacent rooms.

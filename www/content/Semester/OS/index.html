Operating Systems

COCSC301 · 3L–0T–2P. All five units are built — 27 lessons, five printable exam sheets, and every scheduling, memory, disk trace and security model worked in full. Every lesson names the exact concepts, algorithms, and numericals for its topic.

Unit I in one line: the OS is a resource manager between hardware and applications, and its central abstraction is the process — created by fork, described by a PCB, moved through five states, and made cheaper by threads. Unit II in one line: once several processes exist, you must decide who runs (scheduling) and who touches the shared data (synchronisation).

Short on time? Go straight to lesson 9 — the scheduling numericals are the guaranteed 10–15 marks in this paper and are purely mechanical. Then 13 (classic synchronisation problems), 11 (Peterson's), 5 (process states and PCB), 12 (semaphores).

## Knowledge — books

All four are named in the syllabus, so questions are drawn from them.

- **Aho, Lam, Sethi and Ullman — _Compilers: Principles, Techniques and Tools_ (2nd ed.), Pearson.**
  **The dragon book, and the closest match to this syllabus by a very long way.** The unit ordering
  is his chapter ordering. `position = initial + rate * 60` is his figure 1.7; the transition diagram
  for relational operators is his figure 3.13; **(a|b)\*abb is his running example through the whole
  of chapter 3**, including the NFA numbering used in Lesson 8. Chapter 1 is Unit I; chapter 3 is Unit II;
  chapter 4 is Unit III; chapters 6 and 7 are Unit IV; chapters 8 and 9 are Unit V.
- **Chattopadhyay, S. — _Compiler Design_, PHI.**
  Written for exactly this kind of Indian university syllabus, and shorter. Better than Aho for the
  **bootstrapping T-diagram** treatment and for phase-vs-pass.
- **Appel, A. W. — _Modern Compiler Implementation in C_**, Cambridge University Press.
  Implementation-focused. Useful if a lab question asks you to actually build something; more than
  the written paper needs.
- **Louden, K. C. — _Compiler Construction: Principles and Practice_**, PWS.
  Good middle ground, with a complete worked compiler. Its treatment of finite automata and of Lex
  is more readable than Aho's.
